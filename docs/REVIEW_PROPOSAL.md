## What v1 proposed
Four hypothesis groups: H1 shared cross-family defaults; H2a guidance tightens in place and amplifies the existing skew, H2b distillation behaves like guidance, H2c family relocates, H2d larger models tighten; H3 subject-conditioned defaults (variance decomposition); H4 product layer. Measurement: all 15 CLIP aspects on a scale anchored by explicit pole requests (2 reference models × 4 seeds, 5,760 images); spread relative to the pooled IQR of all open groups; a sign-permutation test for H1; a mixed model; a "4 of 6 categories" reporting rule; a location–spread plane with arrows as the primary figure.

## Verdict
**Reject as written; salvageable with substantial narrowing.** Significance 7/10, novelty 4/10, soundness 3/10, CG&A venue fit 5/10. The running generation is useful; the interpretation and analysis plan were not defensible.

## Main points (all addressed in v2)
1. **Novelty overstated.** DefaultShift is the strongest threat: it separates direction of moved mass from entropy change (67 of 1,152 cells with small entropy change but large shift), audits background, lighting and viewpoint besides colour, and includes guidance probes. The executor verified these statements against the paper's full text. CompCon (four-model appendix), GRADE (cross-model TVD), DIMCIM (Does-It/Can-It) and the attribute-conditional benchmark (time of day, background) were also understated. Missing: VisDiff, OpenBias, Default Images.
2. **Hypotheses not identifiable.** Keep guidance (two within-model pairs) as the primary intervention; merge cross-model and subject structure into a descriptive question; keep one distillation package case; drop family, scale and product layer as hypotheses. The two families implement guidance differently, so CFG 4 is not a matched dose (verified in the installed diffusers 0.41: Qwen-Image rescales the guided prediction to the norm of the conditional prediction; klein uses the plain linear combination); the klein 4B/9B pair also changes text encoder (to verify).
3. **Measurement.** Reduce to 4–6 validated aspects; composite aspects (tone, composition, quality, mood, style, background/clutter) are hard to interpret. Anchors are reference conditions, not ground truth; compute per reference, propagate uncertainty, keep semantic exclusions, pilot phrasings; suggested 6 × 24 × 2 × 2 × 8 = 4,608 images. The existing human check validates only extreme pairs; the final validation needs graded differences and the new models. A second encoder is a sensitivity check, not validation.
4. **Statistics.** Pooled-spread normalization favours divergent defaults and hides universally shared tight ones; sign-flip permutation is degenerate; regressing change on baseline is mathematically coupled; "tighten in place" needs equivalence margins; midpoint crossings do not show interaction; "4 of 6 categories" is not evidence control; use a hierarchical bootstrap and a small set of pre-declared primary tests.
5. **Visualization and venue.** The location–spread plane is a useful conventional scatterplot, not an indispensable contribution. Frame the paper as *Visual Auditing of Appearance Defaults in Text-to-Image Models*: an evaluated workflow for three tasks (displacement vs concentration; pooled vs per-subject; trace to images and catch measurement or attribution errors), with experts and a non-trivial baseline (aligned box plots or score table plus image grid).
6. **Plan.** Freeze the narrower questions and margins now; finish authorized generation; validation and anchors in week 2; analysis and draft by Nov 1; no new scope after that.

## Main points
1. **Novelty.** Without calibration the contribution is an integrated audit workflow whose components are familiar (CLIP text directions, location/spread summaries, small multiples, linked images, provenance warnings). Enough for an applied CG&A feature only if the paper shows the combination enables judgements the alternatives do not. Expected reviewer sentence: *"a conventional linked dashboard around noisy CLIP attributes, with a guidance result already predictable from prior work."* Answer with evidence: one complete image-verified audit where pooled and per-subject readings differ, experts finding and correcting it under a fair baseline, and the measurement's failure modes shown next to its successes.
2. **Measurement.** Round 1 is a screen for gross directional errors, not validation of a quantitative axis (12 extreme pairs per aspect; tone and bg κ 0.29 and 0.33). The annotator exclusion was an owner decision after labelling, not a pre-set rule: report the four-annotator results prominently. Choosing the five axes (and dropping the passing mood axis) is a data-informed selection. Call tone a *CLIP vintage/sepia response* or *warm, yellowed photographic look*. A CLIP bipolar difference is an ordered proxy, not a perceptual interval scale; the 0.5 margin depends on which configurations defined s_aspect: report raw CLIP effects and a scale sensitivity analysis. **Wording error:** `aspects/score.py` says resizing means native resolution "does not change how the encoder sees it"; resizing only equalizes input size, so Q2/Q3 contrasts across 512 vs 1024 px may carry resolution effects (Q1 holds resolution fixed).
3. **Statistics.** The hierarchical bootstrap does not literally double-count, but its target must be stated (future subjects within these six categories and future seeds, for these configurations). Percentile intervals of a median of 24 noisy medians may have poor coverage: show all 24 effects and a fixed-subject (seed-only) sensitivity interval. The bootstrap p is uncentred (a confidence-distribution approximation, not a null-calibrated test). "Same place" uses unadjusted 90% intervals while "displaced" uses Holm: the verdicts mix corrected and uncorrected claims. "Displaced" requires significance against 0 and a point estimate ≥ 0.5, which does not establish a true effect beyond 0.5 (klein saturation +0.65, interval from +0.43). Q2's subject-dependent rule holds the centre fixed and understates its uncertainty; the "≥ 3 subjects" rules are case finders without multiplicity control.
4. **Number checks.** No mismatch between `primary.csv` and the Q1 table. Counting rules are not stated: per-subject counts in `RESULTS.md` and `PAPER_PROGRESS.md` (saturation toward vivid 15 / 10, tone toward modern 10 / 9) are point estimates ≥ 0.5; with the interval rule used elsewhere (≥ 0.5 and the 90% interval excluding 0) they are 14 / 10 and 8 / 8 (executor verified). "Robust when any one category is left out" rests on point medians only. `PAPER_DRAFT.md` still reports 11 of 12 configurations and a pending Q1 (executor verified). "Pre-registered" for Q2/Q3 must always carry the disclosure.
5. **Interpretation.** "Guidance mainly concentrates appearance" is too broad. Defensible: *in 24 underspecified prompts, raising guidance from 1 to 4 reduced the spread of several CLIP-measured appearance attributes in FLUX.2 klein-base 4B; Qwen-Image-2512 showed a smaller reduction, clearest in saturation.* Qwen has one Holm-significant concentration; the two pipelines are different interventions; no dose-response from two levels; five proxies do not cover appearance as a whole.
6. **Visualization.** The audit path is right but the screen asks too much. The configuration map's x is relative to each subject's cross-configuration median, not an absolute default; arrows can suggest causal progression for packages; edge triangles may hide the heterogeneity the paper claims to expose. The contrast-overview glyph needs a visible legend and values. Tooltip-only numbers do not work in print. The tool's 400-replicate intervals differ from the analysis's 10,000. The t-SNE map with contours from 32 images per configuration must not be read as concentration: remove the contours and keep it out of the paper. Rank-matched dashed "trajectories" for different checkpoints are quantile alignment, not trajectories: relabel or remove. Keep for the paper: one entry view (contrast overview or configuration map), the five contrast scatters with margins, the reversal matrix, aligned image strips with settings chips; one per-subject distribution in a case figure.
7. **Expert evaluation.** Circular as an accuracy test (authors' group, author-chosen cases, expected answers from the same CLIP summaries). The unsorted grid baseline is too weak: use aligned box plots plus a sortable image grid with the same provenance. Counterbalance case difficulty; record prior involvement; have an independent person judge a sample of answers from the images, blind to condition and CLIP. Include outside experts if at all possible, otherwise call it a formative qualitative evaluation. 45 minutes is not enough for the listed parts (about 63 minutes; executor verified).
8. **Integrity.** Deviations are recorded unusually well; no sign of concealed post-result changes to Q1. But "Q1 is the only blind analysis" overstates: margins and axes were chosen after pilot and round-1 information. Score tables and images are outside the repository, so several claims (byte-identical scripts, provenance) rest on documentation: keep hashes, frozen files, score tables and a manifest.
